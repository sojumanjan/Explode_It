Create the complete ground layer for one 2D top-down action game map, as a single square image. This image will be placed under all buildings, hedges, trees and characters inside the game engine, so it must contain ONLY the ground surface.

LAYOUT (most important rule)
The attached zone map is an exact floor plan. Copy every shape at exactly the same position, size, width and curve. Do not move, straighten, simplify, add or remove any path or lawn. The colors in the zone map are already close to the final colors.
Zone key:
- Light tan (the background everywhere, including the inside of both path rings) = loose sandy dirt
- Slightly darker tan bands = worn footpaths, including two ring-shaped paths (one around the small round lawn, one larger ring in the lower right)
- Dark green circle = the only lawn, a small round lawn inside the upper ring path
There is no plaza and no paving anywhere. The area inside the lower-right ring is plain dirt.

SCALE
The image covers a 64 x 64 meter royal garden. Paths are about 2 meters wide. A character is about 2 meters tall, about 1/32 of the image width. Keep all small details (pebbles, grass blades, cracks) small at that scale: a pebble is roughly the size of a character's foot.

THE PATHS MUST BE SUBTLE (very important)
The paths are NOT gravel, NOT cream, NOT lighter than the ground. They are the same sandy earth, only packed down by many footsteps: one small step DARKER and slightly smoother than the dirt around them, with the same hue. The difference should be gentle, readable when you look for it but never loud.
The edge between path and dirt is soft and irregular: no outline, no rim, no border stones, no sharp line. The packed earth fades into the loose dirt over a short distance, with a few scattered footprints, small pebbles and faint scuff marks along the edge. Where paths meet, they merge naturally.
The paths are completely flat, NOT sunken and NOT raised: no darker band along the upper edge, no lighter band along the lower edge, no shading that makes a path look like a groove, trench or ridge. Both edges of every path look exactly the same.

OTHER ZONES
1. Loose dirt: warm, light sandy earth of a sunny garden, dry. Large soft irregular patches in 2-3 very close tones, a few faint cracks, very sparse tiny pebbles. Calm and almost empty.
2. Lawn: short, dense olive-green grass, a little darker than the dirt but not heavy (muted, not lime, not yellow). Two close tones of green in soft patches. Edges are slightly ragged, with small grass tufts spilling onto the path.

VIEW AND LIGHT
Camera looks down from above at a slight angle, like a classic top-down adventure game. The ground is drawn flat (no perspective, no vanishing point). Small raised details (pebbles, grass tufts) show a tiny darker side on their bottom-right, because light comes from the top-left. No cast shadows from objects that are not in this image.

COLOR AND VALUE
Muted, natural, slightly desaturated colors, in a medium-light value range: a bright, pleasant daytime ground, but not glaring and never near white. Match the brightness of the attached zone map closely; do not darken it. Very low contrast inside each zone so that colorful characters, enemies and bomb effects stand out clearly. Paths a little darker than the dirt. No pure white, no pure black.

STYLE
Clean hand-painted 2D game art matching the attached character sprite: large flat color areas, crisp small details, 2-3 shade steps per material, simple shapes. No gradients across large areas, no airbrush, no blur, no glow, no photo texture, no noisy grain, no visible pixel grid (it will be converted to pixel art later).

DO NOT DRAW
No buildings, walls, hedges, trees, bushes, fences, benches, fountains, statues, gazebos, plazas, paving stones, carts, barrels, banners, water, bridges, characters, animals, UI, text, labels, arrows, grid lines, borders, frames or vignette. No outlines around any zone. No shadows of missing objects. Do not leave blank areas: every pixel is ground.

OUTPUT
Square image, ground filled edge to edge, the same framing as the zone map (top of the zone map = top of the image).
